# Life RPG — Wandering Mercenary: Character Sheet v1

**Working identifier:** The Wandering Mercenary (true name undecided)
**Status:** Core personality, relationship premise, signature sound, gait, weapon category and hooded visual direction approved in discussion. Exact biography, identity, dimensions, chronology, combat mechanics, woman’s identity and fate are unresolved. Visual illustrations are reference concepts, not automatic canon.
**Narrative role:** Feared roaming mercenary, recurring antagonist the player will fight; potential morally complicated tragedy. Not confirmed as the main villain or guild member.
**References:** Life RPG Complete Story Bible; Story Development and Integration Guide; Character Visual Sheets v1; latest concept artwork.

## Core dramatic contradiction
A notorious killer who has butchered hundreds, including innocents, and whose appetite for violence is genuine. He is never irritable or theatrically enraged: he is eerily calm, economical, exhausted, and terrifying to look at. He genuinely loves a woman who knows only his gentle, attentive side. He stays apart because he believes his presence, history and enemies would destroy her life and reputation. His affection does not excuse his crimes. The woman has her own agency and life beyond him.

## Character identity and psychological design
- **Emotional baseline:** Unshakably calm, even in threat or pain. Never a hotheaded brute. No shouting, threats, villainous laughter or performative intimidation.
- **Bloodlust:** A real appetite for killing, not merely a rumor or an uncontrollable rage. He can enjoy violence without changing his expression. Avoid supernatural explanations until separately approved.
- **Eyes:** Heavy-lidded, fixed, deeply predatory, disquieting even to people unaware of his identity. The gaze conveys bloodlust without cartoon red glow. Some concepts depict reddish irises; treat this as lighting/art direction unless approved as a physical trait.
- **Voice:** Low, quiet, measured, sparse; ordinary politeness is more unsettling than menacing dialogue.
- **Social behavior:** Does not announce himself, posture, threaten, brag, or correct rumors. His physical presence is enough to silence a room.
- **Ethics:** His atrocities are real. Tenderness toward one person must not turn him into an innocent misunderstood hero. Do not use romantic love as automatic redemption.
- **Internal tension:** He feels undeserving of the woman; his distance is motivated by danger, shame and a desire not to expose her to association with him. His exact self-awareness and guilt remain to be written.

## Distinctive physical design
- **Build:** Tall, imposing, broad shoulders and strong arms from sustained combat; not an idealized bodybuilder. Weight and exhaustion visible in posture.
- **Face:** Weathered, gaunt, prominent cheekbones, old scars, sunken/heavy eyes; relaxed mouth and brow, *not* an angry scowl.
- **Hair:** Dark, long, uneven, streaked grey; roughly tied back into a low knot or loose tied mass beneath the hood, with a deliberately exposed uneven hairline where visible. This remains a visual proposal, not fixed canon.
- **Skin:** Battle-worn, old scars, uneven complexion, not polished or glamorous.
- **Silhouette:** High ragged hood, sloping asymmetric shoulders, hanging layers, one heavy armored leg leading, one arm low with immense blade trailing diagonally to ground. Recognizable at thumbnail scale.
- **Clothes:** Heavy, tattered hooded tarp/poncho or travel cloak over mismatched battered metal armor, repaired leather straps, torn charcoal and muted crimson cloth, practical worn boots; not ceremonial or ornate.
- **Palette:** Charcoal, iron, soot, dried-blood burgundy, tarnished brown; burnt orange/crimson primarily as dramatic lighting, not proof of magical powers.
- **Condition:** Old repairs, scratched plates, dirt, frayed seams and worn leather. Material details must be functional and coherent.

## Gait and movement — defining animation identity
- Moves **slowly**, heavily, tiredly, with an uneven almost-limping step.
- The enormous sword burdens his arm/shoulder and is **dragged along the ground**, not worn across his back while walking.
- The tip/edge scrapes stone, dirt, thresholds or floorboards; rhythm varies with surface and his irregular steps.
- The dragging is not a theatrical threat: he is exhausted from carrying the weapon, yet refuses to abandon it.
- His walking posture remains asymmetric and believable. Old injuries may contribute; precise injury not yet canon.
- Combat movement is controlled and brutally efficient, not a screaming berserker transformation. He may straighten and lift the sword, revealing the power concealed by his weary gait.
- Do not magically erase his limp or physical limitations; choreography should account for them.

## Weapon specification
**Type:** Monumental, oversized **two-handed greatsword/longsword-inspired weapon** with a continuous solid steel blade. It is much larger than a standard historical longsword, with a distinctly long two-hand grip.
- **NO** hole, cutout, ring-shaped opening or cleaver-like perforation in blade.
- Strong, continuous blade silhouette; battered straight edges and worn tip from prolonged dragging.
- Visible crossguard, long wrapped grip and secure pommel; believable tang/grip alignment.
- Heavy scratched metal with nicks, oxidation, abrasions and repaired damage; avoid excessive decorative fantasy ornament.
- The sword must be capable of contacting ground at a believable angle while his arm hangs down; do not make it appear weightless.
- In walking pose, **one hand** may hold the long grip near the guard while the other hangs free; **two hands** grip it during a deliberate combat swing. The phrase 'two-handed sword' describes the weapon's intended use, not a requirement to hold it with both hands every second.
- **Anatomy QA:** natural wrist pronation/supination, elbow orientation, thumb wrapped around grip, finger placement and knuckles aligned, hilt and blade share a consistent axis; no impossible backward hand twist, disconnected hilt or floating sword. Show clear contact point and weight distribution.
- The sword's origin, name, exact dimensions, and reason he never leaves it behind are **open mysteries**, not yet established.

## Signature sound and environmental staging
His signature is the **long abrasive scrape of steel**, interrupted by a slow uneven step:
> Scrrrrrrr… step. Scrrrrrrr… step.
- Villagers hear him **before** seeing him; a conversation stalls, people shift aside, a room goes silent.
- They need not know his identity. They are frozen by his look, gait, sword and gaze.
- No NPC should casually ask whether he is the famous mercenary to manufacture exposition.
- When he sits and rests the blade, the scraping stops; the absence of the sound is meaningful.
- In a later dark scene, sound alone could identify his arrival.
- At the woman's home, proposed subtle detail: he leaves the sword outside. This is a **story beat proposal**, not confirmed canon.

## Public vs private behavior
**In public:** Polite if spoken to, usually silent, emotionally flat, indifferent to fear. Not aggressive for spectacle. People instinctively stop talking and avoid his path.
**In combat:** Fearsome skill, genuine bloodlust, controlled breath and expression, no anger. The player must eventually fight him; reason, number of encounters and outcome remain undecided.
**With the woman:** The same quiet man, but patient, considerate, gently attentive; his eyes soften rather than his entire personality changing. She does not know the full violence of his past and knows him as loving. He avoids publicly associating with her. No relationship exposition in dialogue or captions.

## Relationship / tragedy development boundaries
- Woman: adult, independent character, not yet named, no confirmed occupation, age or residence. She must have personal goals, judgments and choices.
- He loves her but believes he can neither marry nor openly be with her without staining or endangering her life. Her perceived 'purity' is **his subjective idealization**, not an objective statement that she lacks agency or moral complexity.
- He hides his reputation and past. What name she knows him by remains undecided.
- Her discovery of his crimes, whether she forgives him, and whether he dies are **not finalized**.
- Desired emotional register: the painful contradiction of genuine love in a brutal killer; inspiration from the emotional effect of Zabuza and Haku, **without copying** their relationship, weapon or plot.
- Avoid an easy last-minute redemption speech or forgiveness. If he dies, it must be earned by preceding choices and consequences.

## Narrative placement and mystery
- Enters the village as a wandering stranger, without dramatic announcement; villagers go quiet because of his presence and scraping sword.
- The player encounters and ultimately fights him; cause of conflict, escalation and possible repeated encounters are open.
- Possible ties to a razed settlement, hidden contracts or lost guild are **unapproved exploratory ideas**, not established history.
- He may have secrets independent of the central mystery; do not force every mystery into one conspiracy.
- His notoriety, body count and crimes are part of the design premise; specific massacres, victims, dates and employers must be developed before entering canon.

## Visual art direction and asset requirements
- Dark-fantasy digital oil painting; expressive textured brushstrokes, semi-realistic anatomy, dramatic chiaroscuro, atmospheric shadows, burnt-orange/crimson accents, weathered metallic surfaces, cinematic gritty composition.
- **No photorealism, clean vector outlines, superhero poses, magical glowing eyes by default, or generic fantasy handsome-man face.**
- Distinct haircut and silhouette per global character-art rule.
- For game character cutouts: full body, sword tip and feet fully within frame, genuine transparent PNG alpha, no painted checkerboard baked into pixels.
- For presentation/reference sheets: front/side/back silhouettes, face/eye close-ups, hood up/down, weapon close-up, dragging walk, two-handed combat grip, texture studies, annotations separate from gameplay cutout.
- Confirm hand direction, elbow, grip and sword-ground geometry before approval.
- Preserve design across portraits, idle poses, movement animation and story scenes once approved.

## Continuity checklist for future writing and art
1. Does he remain calm rather than angry?
2. Does his gaze frighten even someone who does not know his name?
3. Is his slow uneven walk and steel scrape present when appropriate?
4. Is the weapon a solid, truly two-handed giant sword with correct anatomy and physical weight?
5. Is his hooded cloak a practical, torn garment rather than costume decoration?
6. Are the woman's agency and ignorance of his past preserved until an earned discovery?
7. Does his tenderness coexist with, rather than erase, culpability for atrocities?
8. Are clues shown through witnessed behavior, artifacts and natural dialogue, never narrator exposition?
9. Are unapproved plot possibilities clearly marked as proposals?
10. Are transparent cutouts truly alpha-transparent and the full blade visible?
